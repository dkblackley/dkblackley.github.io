It might seem easy to write, I'm willing to bet most won't even remember the process of learning this seemingly simple task. Writing is no problem, *writing well* is. If you have found this article, I assume you're hoping I can give you the secret to writing well, but I can't. What I can provide is the tips I've picked up writing papers throughout my PhD. To summarize, this post aims to provide help to those who need to write:

- Dense academic writing, with a focus on mathematics and theorems.
- In a way that can be understood on first read.
- Concisely, or under a limited number of words or pages.

Though I'm focussing on academia, I won't be telling you how to write LaTeX. A fantastic style guide (and for many good tips on how to write well) I would recommend [the Stanford style guide for LaTeX](https://web.stanford.edu/class/ee364b/latex_templates/template_notes.pdf) which focusses on how to write math well in LaTeX as well as many tips for writing well in general.


# Be consistent
As a new PhD student, it may be tempting to flex your vocabulary at a reader. If you want to write well you should ensure that any nouns you place are only appearing for this first time if they have not been referenced before. Consider the following excerpt:

`This section discusses sparse & dense retrieval architectures for RAG, with particular attention to the challenges and trade-offs that arise when adapting each paradigm to meet privacy-preserving requirements through PIR.`

Though understandable, it is not *consistent*.
